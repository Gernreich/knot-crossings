Knot Crossings

← All projects · Turn the ropes in 3D →

Two ropes, drawn once and crossing each other 8 times. Every crossing can go either way, over or under, so the one drawing holds 28 = 256 knots. The author drew all 256 by hand on yellow legal paper, numbered 0 to 255, and marked each crossing that goes the other way with a small circle. The circles are the number written in binary, crossing 1 for 1, crossing 2 for 2, crossing 3 for 4, and so on up to crossing 8 for 128. The knots the author recognised are listed in an index: the square knot, the sheet bend, the Carrick bend and its relatives.

Here knot_crossings.py works the 256 out again from the key, and sorts them into knots with the Kauffman bracket, a number-like invariant that stays the same however a knot is redrawn. Without a circle, a crossing goes over for the rope that reaches it first, following the 1st rope and then the 2nd. Under this reading the Carrick bend, number 117, and its mirror image 138 = 255 − 117 are the only two that go over, under, over, under all the way along both ropes. Reading every crossing the other way gives the mirror image of every drawing, and changes no name.

The index, checked

The square knot, which is its own mirror image, lands in a group that is its own mirror, and the sheet bend in a group and its mirror image. No drawing up to 122, the last one indexed, is in a named group without being named. Three pairs of names turn out to be mirror images of one another, and two of those pairs clash: 109, a single carrick bend (reformed constrictor) in the index, is the mirror image of 73, a whatnot; and 118, a Carrick bend, is the mirror image of 74, a single carrick bend. A mirror image keeps its name, and the names in The Ashley Book of Knots (below) settle both: 109 is a whatnot and 118 a single carrick bend. Read that way, the reformed constrictor is one knot, 77 and 105, and no two mirror images carry the names of different knots (106 and 114 differ only by “unknown”). The index itself is kept as written.

The groups

Each group holds the drawings with the same Kauffman bracket, counting two drawings as one knot when they differ only by the ends of a rope being turned about each other, as a rope’s two ends can be in the hand. Different brackets prove different knots; the same bracket strongly suggests, but does not prove, the same knot. |lk| is how many times the ropes wind round each other when each is closed on itself. The drawing shown is the group’s first. Names in quotes are written beside the drawings rather than in the index.

Closing the ends

A rope with two loose ends can always be untied, so to ask whether a drawing is really knotted its ends have to be joined. The four ends lie round the outside of the drawing, and there are two ways to join them without making a new crossing: each rope’s end back to its own start, which gives two closed loops, a link; or each rope’s end on to the other rope’s start, which gives one closed loop, a knot. Every drawing is closed both ways. Shown here with the Carrick bend, 117.

Each closure is named by its Jones polynomial, which here differs between every knot and link that turns up, and checked with its Alexander polynomial and determinant, which must agree with it. The names were also checked against SnapPy, which also untangles every unlinked pair to no crossings at all. Knot names are from the Rolfsen table, with * marking the mirror image of the knot without it; link names are Thistlethwaite’s. The square knot and 820 share their Alexander polynomial and determinant; only the Jones polynomial tells them apart. A link is the same link whichever way its ropes run, but its polynomials are not, so a link can show more than one. Mirror-image drawings, n and 255 − n, always close to mirror images.

Names in the book

The Ashley Book of Knots (1944) works from the same diagram. Ashley draws the reef knot, sheet bend, Carrick bend and granny knot on it (#1549–1552) and notes that an eight-crossing knot allows 256 over-and-under arrangements. In 2025, Eric (NautiKnots on the forum of the International Guild of Knot Tyers) tied all of them, each with the standing ends on the same side and on opposite sides, and named every bend that held from Ashley: Bends Based on the Carrick Bend Diagram. Which end is standing changes how the knot is pulled, not the drawing, so one drawing can carry two names, one for each lead. The names below are Eric’s, with one more from knotsaver in the same thread.

Eric’s crossings A to H are crossings 6, 1, 5, 8, 7, 4, 3 and 2 of the key. Eric’s figures of the Thief Knot, the Carrick Bend and the third Single Carrick Bend, read crossing by crossing, come out as drawings here, each in the group this page had already put it in. The other figures no longer load on the forum, so the rest are matched by elimination: Eric finds three Single Carrick Bends and two Whatnots, and ties the Whatnot 8 ways and the Thief Knot 12, which fits only one way. The count agrees with this page: of the 512 bends, 100 held and the rest fell apart, and groups 1 to 6 here hold 206 drawings, which leaves 50 knotted drawings, 100 with both leads. The Ashley numbers are Eric’s, and Wikipedia’s where it gives them, not checked against the book.
